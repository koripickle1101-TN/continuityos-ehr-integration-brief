# Workflow Analysis

## Current Workflow Problem

Disconnected healthcare information systems can create avoidable friction across the patient journey. When intake, clinical documentation, coding, and billing do not operate as one connected workflow, patients may repeat information, staff may duplicate work, and revenue cycle teams may receive incomplete or inconsistent documentation.

## Workflow Stages

1. Patient check-in
2. Registration and demographic verification
3. Insurance verification
4. Clinical documentation
5. Orders and care coordination
6. Coding review
7. Charge capture
8. Claim submission
9. Denial management
10. Patient billing and follow-up

## Likely Breakdown Points

- Duplicate data entry
- Incomplete intake information
- Missing documentation
- Delayed coding review
- Poor handoffs between clinical and billing teams
- Lack of real-time workflow visibility
- Staff workarounds outside the system

## Patient Impact

Patients may experience longer waits, repeated questions, delayed care coordination, confusing bills, and frustration when departments do not appear connected.

## Staff Impact

Staff may experience rework, alert fatigue, documentation burden, unclear accountability, and workflow stress.

## Revenue Cycle Impact

The organization may experience increased claim denials, delayed reimbursement, higher days in accounts receivable, and more manual reconciliation work.

## ContinuityOS Framing

ContinuityOS frames the solution around one principle: the patient journey and the revenue cycle are connected. Intake accuracy, documentation quality, coding clarity, and billing performance should be treated as one continuous operational system rather than separate departmental tasks.
