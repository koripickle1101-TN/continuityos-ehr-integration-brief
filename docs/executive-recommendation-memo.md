# Executive Recommendation Memo

## Subject

Recommendation for Improving Health Information System Integration

## Author

Kori Pickle  
Health Care Management Student  
Patient-to-Professional Perspective

## Simulation Note

This recommendation is written as a simulated executive communication for academic and portfolio purposes. It does not represent employment experience, internal access, or confidential organizational information.

---

Dear CIO,

As a health care management student with extensive patient experience, I have seen how information systems directly affect what patients feel at the bedside, at the front desk, and later when they receive a bill. When registration, documentation, coding, and billing are disconnected, patients wait longer, repeat information, experience confusion, and may face delays in care. Staff also feel the burden because they must work around system gaps while still trying to provide safe and efficient service.

Based on what I have learned in health care information systems coursework, I recommend moving toward a more fully integrated enterprise information system that connects patient intake, clinical documentation, coding, and billing into one continuous workflow. The goal is not simply to add more technology. The goal is to improve continuity, reduce rework, support staff, and create a smoother patient experience.

The first step should be workflow discovery. This means mapping the process from check-in through discharge and billing while listening to front-desk staff, nurses, providers, coders, billing specialists, and patient-facing teams. The organization should identify where duplicate entry, missing documentation, unclear handoffs, and delayed communication occur.

After the current workflow is clearly understood, the organization can define clinical, operational, and revenue cycle requirements. These requirements should be used to evaluate available EHR modules, communication tools, patient flow solutions, and revenue cycle support features. A structured scorecard should compare usability, integration, patient impact, implementation support, and long-term operational value.

Implementation should be phased. The system should be configured and tested using realistic patient scenarios before go-live. Staff should receive role-specific training, and go-live should include strong at-the-elbow support so employees can focus on patients while learning the new workflow.

After implementation, the system should be continuously optimized. Feedback from the units, clinics, coding teams, billing teams, and patients should guide adjustments to documentation templates, order sets, alerts, reports, and communication pathways.

If done well, patients should experience fewer repeated questions, shorter delays, clearer communication, and a more coordinated care journey. Clinicians should spend less time fighting the EHR and more time interacting with patients. Coding and billing teams should benefit from clearer documentation, fewer claim denials, and faster reimbursement.

Success should be measured with patient, staff, operational, and financial indicators. Recommended metrics include patient wait time, documentation completeness, clinician satisfaction, room turnover time, claim denial rate, days in accounts receivable, clean claim rate, and coding query volume. These measures should be reviewed at 30, 90, and 180 days after implementation.

This recommendation reflects staged acquisition and implementation concepts from health care information systems coursework, applied through a patient-to-professional lens.

Sincerely,  
Kori Pickle  
Health Care Management Student
